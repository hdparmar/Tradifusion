Tradi-fusion

Riffusion fine-tuned on Irish traditional music

My master's thesis at KTH, supervised by Bob L. T. Sturm. Riffusion is Stable Diffusion taught to draw music as spectrograms. I fine-tuned it on about 9,000 spectrograms cut from recordings of Irish traditional music, then asked it for jigs, reels and airs.

Everything below was generated in October 2026, on a laptop GPU, with the published model and the pipeline in this repository: 19 clips of 5.12 seconds, all of them, unedited apart from matching their volume. The settings for each are in experiments.json.

▸ 01Text to audio

One prompt, one 5-second clip. Every training image had the same caption, An Irish Traditional Tune, so the words mostly nudge the result; the seed spectrogram it starts from shapes it more. Click a frame to play it.

▸ 02How it works

  1. S1

    Cut

    About 1,700 recordings from Bob L. T. Sturm's collection, made mono at 44.1 kHz and cut into 5.12-second pieces, with silence removed.

  2. S2

    Draw

    Each piece becomes a 512 × 512 picture: a mel spectrogram with 512 bands from 60 to 8,500 Hz, 100 ms Hann windows, 10 ms apart. Dark means loud.

  3. S3

    Caption

    Every picture gets the same caption, An Irish Traditional Tune: about 9,000 image and text pairs.

  4. S4

    Fine-tune

    Starting from Riffusion v1, the U-Net trains for 122k steps (202 epochs) on two RTX A6000s, about four days. The CLIP text encoder stays frozen.

  5. S5

    Generate

    A real spectrogram from the dataset is encoded, noised (denoising 0.75) and denoised over 50 steps, steered by the prompt. Sliding α between prompts or seeds gives the next frame.

  6. S6

    Listen

    Inverse mel scale, then 32 rounds of Griffin-Lim to guess the phase, then a Tukey window on the first and last 100 ms. Frames are joined end to end.

▸ 03The 2024 examples

The clips this page launched with in March 2024, made on the CPU of this same laptop at about ten minutes each. Their spectrograms are redrawn here from the audio.

▸ 04What it can't do

  • It doesn't write tunes. Each frame is 5 seconds with no idea of the AABB form of a reel or jig. Long pieces are frames placed side by side.
  • Griffin-Lim guesses the phase, so the sound is smeared and a little metallic. A trained vocoder (MelGAN, HiFi-GAN) would sound much cleaner.
  • The prompt is a weak handle. With one caption for all 9,000 images, words like banjo or harp come from Stable Diffusion's text encoder rather than from the music. The seed image matters more.
  • Joins are audible. Every frame is faded in and out over 100 ms before it is joined to the next.
  • The recordings stay private. The model learned from commercial recordings, so only its outputs are published here, not the music it learned from.
